Outside the Magistrate's Shadow: Media Beyond Local Authority and Cadre Promotion in China

Leo Yang Working paper · 2026

Linking fatal mining accidents, news coverage, and officials’ careers, I examine which media channels constrain local authority. Career penalties are associated with coverage from outside an official’s province, rather than local reporting.

User Location Disclosure Fails to Deter Overseas Criticism but Amplifies Regional Divisions on Chinese Social Media

Leo Yang, Yiqing Xu Working paper · 2025

We study the rollout of location labels on Weibo. Disclosure did not deter overseas engagement, but it reduced domestic participation in discussions outside users’ home provinces and amplified regional discrimination.

Gaslighting: Comment Moderation on Chinese Social Media

Thomas Cao, Yiqing Xu, Leo Yang Working paper · 2023

We study how government-affiliated Weibo accounts combine propaganda with comment moderation. High-frequency data and survey experiments examine the effects on engagement, remaining content, and beliefs about public opinion.

Do Winners Spread More Words? Factional Competition and Local Media Reports on Negative News in Autocracy

Ji Yeon Hong, Leo Yang Political Science Research and Methods · 2022

Using Chinese newspaper coverage and elite networks, we examine how factional competition shapes reporting of negative political news. Officials connected to stronger national leaders tend to criticize weaker factions, with consequences for promotion and the concentration of power.
